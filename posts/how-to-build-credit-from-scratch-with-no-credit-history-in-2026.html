How to Build Credit From Scratch With No History (2026)

Last updated October 28, 2026. Plan prices and limits change, so confirm current terms on Kikoff's site before you sign up.

With no credit history, lenders have nothing to evaluate, so you are "credit invisible" rather than "bad." The fix is to create a file with at least one account that reports on-time payments. This guide covers how to get started and what to expect in the first year.

Step 1: Check That You Really Have No File

Pull your free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com. If they are empty, you are starting fresh. If you see accounts you do not recognize, treat that as possible identity theft and dispute them first.

Step 2: Pick Your First Account

You have four sensible starting points:

  • Kikoff credit account. No credit check, plans from $5 a month, reports to all three bureaus. See how it works.
  • Secured card. A refundable deposit becomes your limit. You get true revolving history.
  • Credit-builder loan. Fixed payments held in savings. See how these loans work.
  • Authorized user. A relative with a long good history adds you to a card.

Our options roundup helps you decide.

Step 3: Add Rent Reporting

If you pay rent, ask your landlord's system or a rent-reporting service to report it. It adds months of history without a new debt.

Step 4: Pay On Time, Every Time

Payment history is the biggest score factor. Use autopay for the full statement balance on any card and at least the minimum on everything else.

Step 5: Keep Utilization Low

When you get a card, use a small purchase each month and pay it off. Keeping reported balances under 10 to 30 percent of your limit protects your score.

Step 6: Wait Before Adding More

Give your first account three to six months, then consider one more product, such as an unsecured starter card, if your score has risen. Every application adds a hard inquiry, so space them out.

What to Expect

You will usually see a score appear after three to six months of reporting. Scoring models need enough activity to calculate one, so a first score is common after the first few cycles. The number varies with the product mix and how consistently you pay, and no provider can promise a specific result. For the broader playbook, see the ultimate guide to raising your score.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to build credit from nothing?

Most people see a first score in three to six months of on-time reporting. Reaching a good score typically takes a year or more of consistent behavior.

What is the fastest way to start?

Opening a Kikoff account, a secured card or becoming an authorized user can all start your file within a billing cycle or two.

Is no credit the same as bad credit?

No. No credit means there is nothing to score. Bad credit means there is negative history. Both can be fixed with consistent on-time payments.
