5 Best Credit Builder Options to Improve Your Score in 2026

Last updated October 28, 2026. Plan prices and limits change, so confirm current terms on Kikoff's site before you sign up.

"Credit builder" covers several different products, and the best one depends on whether you have cash for a deposit, how much you want to spend each month and which bureaus you need to reach. Here are five common options, what each costs and who it fits. Always verify current fees on the provider's own site, since pricing changes.

1. Kikoff Credit Account

Kikoff is a revolving credit line you can use only for Kikoff's own fee and its digital store, so it works as a builder rather than a spending tool. Plans run $5, $20 and $35 a month with limits of $750, $2,500 and $3,500 respectively, there is no credit check, and activity reports to Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. Best for people with little or no credit who want the lowest monthly cost. Read the full pros and cons before choosing a tier.

2. Credit-Builder Loans (Self, Credit Union Versions)

With a credit-builder loan, the lender holds the money in a savings account while you make fixed payments. When the term ends, you receive the savings, less interest and fees. This adds an installment loan to your mix and forces savings. The catch is the cost of interest and fees, and a missed payment hurts. Our guide explains how credit-builder loans work step by step.

3. Secured Credit Cards

You put down a refundable deposit that becomes your limit, then use the card like any other and pay the statement. Good secured cards report to all three bureaus, have no annual fee and graduate you to an unsecured card. Best if you can spare $200 to $500 and want real revolving practice. See credit-builder loans versus secured cards for the detailed comparison.

4. Authorized-User Tradelines

A trusted relative with a long, clean card account adds you as an authorized user. Their history can appear on your report, which helps your average account age. It costs nothing but carries risk: their late payments or high balances can hurt you too, and not every issuer reports authorized-user accounts to every bureau. Never buy authorized-user spots from a stranger, as it can violate issuer terms.

5. Rent and Bill Reporting

Services that report on-time rent payments add history for something you already pay. Some builder plans bundle it, and standalone services charge a monthly or one-time fee. Confirm which bureaus receive the data and whether the lenders you plan to use will count it.

How to Choose

If cash is tight, start with Kikoff or rent reporting. If you can save a deposit, a secured card adds revolving history that most lenders like. If you struggle to save, a credit-builder loan enforces it. Many people combine two: a low-cost builder now and a secured or starter card later. For a head-to-head view, see our Kikoff comparison with other builder tools.

Mistakes to Avoid

Do not stack several builder products at once, since each paid plan and inquiry adds cost. Do not assume a builder account will lift a score by a set number of points. Do not skip autopay. And cancel anything you no longer need only after checking how closing it affects your account age.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest way to build credit?

Kikoff's $5 plan and authorized-user status on a family member's good account are the lowest-cost routes. Neither requires a deposit.

Do credit builder products report to all three bureaus?

Many do, but not all. Kikoff reports to Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. Check each provider before you sign up.

How long until a credit builder helps my score?

Expect three to six months of on-time reporting before the account makes a visible difference, and more if your file has negative items.
